Chapter Four Communication and the Things We Leave Unsaid Communication is often discussed as though the greatest challenge in a relationship is finding the right words, but sometimes the greater difficulty is finding the willingness to say what needs to be said. People may recognize that something is bothering them, understand that a conversation would help, and still choose to remain quiet because they are uncertain how the other person will respond. They may want to avoid another disagreement, protect an otherwise peaceful evening, or wait until they feel more comfortable expressing themselves. Over time, however, the things left unsaid can become just as influential as the conversations that actually take place. The two men had experienced this in different ways throughout their relationship. One was more likely to address a concern as soon as he recognized something was bothering him, while the other sometimes needed time to think before discussing an uncomfortable subject. Neither approach was automatically unhealthy, although the difference occasionally left one waiting for a conversation that the other believed could be postponed. One evening, they were discussing their plans for the upcoming weekend. The father mentioned that he needed to make time for his son, while his partner began thinking about the other commitments they had already discussed. There was no disagreement about the importance of parenting, and his partner understood that plans involving a child could change. What he had not mentioned was that he had been looking forward to having some uninterrupted time together, particularly after a busy week. Rather than explaining that expectation, he simply agreed with the arrangements and continued the conversation. The father interpreted his agreement as an indication that everything was settled, while his partner quietly hoped that he would recognize the disappointment he had not expressed. Neither realized that they had walked away from the discussion with different understandings of what had been decided. A few days later, the subject resurfaced when the weekend did not unfold as his partner had imagined. “I thought we were going to have some time together this weekend,” he said. “We did talk about spending time together, but I also told you I needed to be available for my son.” “I know, and I wasn’t expecting you to change that. I just wish I had told you how much I was looking forward to having some time with you.” The father looked at him, realizing that the concern was not simply about the schedule. His partner had agreed to the arrangements without explaining what he was hoping for, and now both were trying to understand why the weekend had become disappointing. “I didn’t know you felt that way,” the father replied. “That’s part of the problem. Sometimes I don’t say anything because I don’t want you to feel like I’m asking you to choose between me and your responsibilities.” The statement opened a more meaningful conversation than the original disagreement. His partner was not asking to take priority over the father’s son, nor was he questioning the importance of their parenting arrangements. He was trying to explain that his own needs sometimes remained unspoken because he worried that expressing them might be interpreted as a lack of understanding. The father had not realized how much his partner had been holding back. From his perspective, the absence of a concern meant there was nothing that needed to be addressed. He had not understood that silence sometimes reflected hesitation rather than satisfaction, and that agreeing to an arrangement did not necessarily mean someone had expressed everything he was feeling. There is a significant difference between giving someone time to process a conversation and leaving that person to guess what remains unresolved. Healthy communication does not require immediate answers to every difficult question, but it does require some indication that the conversation matters and will receive the attention it deserves. When people repeatedly postpone meaningful discussions without returning to them, the other person may begin interpreting that silence as indifference. This can happen in conversations about affection, household responsibilities, personal expectations, and even spiritual matters. Two people may attend the same church and share a commitment to following Jesus, yet avoid discussing questions that affect their individual faith journeys. One may assume the other feels comfortable with a particular interpretation of Scripture, while the other remains uncertain about how to express a different understanding. Even within an affirming congregation, meaningful conversations about faith require honesty, patience, and respect for the fact that spiritual growth is personal. The same is true when discussing the future of a relationship. A person may want reassurance about commitment but hesitate to ask for it, believing that the other should already know what is needed. Someone else may assume that everything is secure because there has been no disagreement about the future. Both may care deeply about the relationship while missing opportunities to understand what the other needs. As the conversation continued, the two men began recognizing that neither was entirely responsible for the misunderstanding. One had not expressed an important expectation, while the other had assumed that agreement meant complete understanding. Moving forward would require both of them to become more intentional about communicating rather than relying on unspoken expectations to guide their decisions. They eventually agreed that needing time to think was perfectly reasonable, provided that important conversations were not simply abandoned. They also recognized that expressing a need did not automatically make someone demanding, and listening to that need did not mean surrendering every personal boundary. Their relationship would benefit from a willingness to speak honestly before disappointment had an opportunity to become resentment. What remains unspoken does not always disappear with time. Sometimes those thoughts become the very things people eventually argue about, even though the original concern could have been addressed much earlier. Learning to communicate means giving another person the opportunity to understand what matters before silence creates a meaning neither person intended. The conversations we avoid can eventually become the misunderstandings we struggle to repair. Sometimes the greatest act of consideration is finding the courage to explain what matters before someone else is left trying to guess.
