Chapter Fourteen Knowing When to Keep Trying There is something beautiful about being loved by someone who continues choosing you after the excitement of the beginning has settled into the familiarity of everyday life. The early stages of a relationship often bring anticipation, butterflies, and the desire to spend every available moment together. Over time, love develops a different kind of beauty. It becomes the hand reaching for yours without being asked, the familiar smile across a room, the unexpected kiss before leaving for work, and the comfort of knowing someone is genuinely happy to see you at the end of the day. For the two men, love had never been limited to the serious conversations they had needed to have. They shared a friendship that remained one of the most valuable parts of their relationship. They knew how to make each other laugh, how to recognize when the other needed encouragement, and how to turn an ordinary evening into something worth remembering. There were inside jokes that made little sense to anyone else, playful disagreements over what to watch, and affectionate moments that reminded them how much they enjoyed simply being together. One Saturday morning, the father woke to find his partner already in the kitchen preparing breakfast. The smell of coffee had filled the house, and music played quietly in the background. When the father walked in, his partner turned around with a smile. “Good morning, handsome.” The father laughed. “You only call me handsome when you want something.” His partner walked over, wrapped his arms around him, and kissed him. “Maybe I just think you’re handsome.” “Well, that’s a much better reason.” They laughed, and the father held him close for another moment before asking what was for breakfast. The exchange was ordinary, but neither man took the affection for granted. They had learned that love did not need a special occasion to be expressed. Sometimes the most meaningful reassurance came through small gestures that communicated, without explanation, that the other person was wanted. Their relationship also had room for the kind of affection that made each feel secure. A hand resting on a shoulder while passing through the room, a kiss on the forehead, an embrace from behind while one prepared dinner, or falling asleep with their hands intertwined were familiar parts of their lives. Physical affection was not something they used to avoid difficult conversations or prove that everything was fine. It was a genuine expression of the closeness they shared, given freely because they enjoyed one another. The father especially appreciated how his partner could bring warmth into a demanding day. There were evenings when work and parenting responsibilities left him mentally exhausted, and he did not always have the energy for a lengthy conversation. His partner had learned to recognize those moments without assuming that quietness meant rejection. Sometimes he offered a hug, sat beside him, or simply asked whether he wanted company. The father, in turn, had become more intentional about returning that affection rather than assuming his partner already knew how much he was loved. Their son remained an important part of the father’s life, and the two men continued respecting the boundaries surrounding his parenting relationship. The partner enjoyed the familiar friendship he had developed with the boy, including shared jokes and ordinary conversations, while understanding that his role was not to replace a parent or claim authority that did not belong to him. The father appreciated the kindness between them and made sure the child’s needs and family relationships remained protected. Their home could hold different kinds of love without requiring anyone to compete for a place in someone else’s life. One afternoon, the father and his partner went out together without a complicated itinerary. They stopped for lunch, browsed through a few stores, and spent more time laughing than they did making decisions about what to buy. At one point, his partner reached for his hand while they walked through the parking lot. The father squeezed his hand and looked over at him. “What?” his partner asked. “Nothing. I’m just glad you’re here.” His partner smiled. “You know, you could say things like that more often.” “I know. I’m working on being more romantic.” “You’re doing a terrible job. I think you need more practice.” The father pulled him closer and kissed him, leaving them both laughing as they continued toward the car. Those moments reminded them that affection was not an unnecessary addition to a serious relationship. Tenderness helped communicate appreciation, belonging, and desire. It gave each man opportunities to experience love without having to ask whether the relationship was still important. They were not trying to impress one another every day. They were enjoying the privilege of sharing life with someone whose presence had become deeply meaningful. There were also moments when their affection carried them through more difficult days. When one received disappointing news, the other did not always need to offer a solution. Sometimes listening, holding him close, and reminding him that he did not have to face every burden alone was enough to begin restoring a sense of comfort. They were learning that companionship was not about having the perfect answer. Often, love was demonstrated through the willingness to remain present while someone worked through something difficult. Their faith was woven naturally into their shared life. They prayed together, attended their affirming church, and encouraged one another in their walk with Jesus. There were mornings when they discussed something they had heard in a sermon, evenings when one reminded the other to make time for prayer, and moments when they simply thanked God for the blessings they had experienced. Their relationship did not have to be hidden or separated from their faith. They were able to worship openly, love one another sincerely, and continue growing in their understanding of what it meant to follow Christ. The father sometimes watched his partner during worship and felt grateful for the life they shared. He loved seeing him sing, hearing him laugh with people in their church community, and recognizing the same kindness that had drawn him toward him in the beginning. His partner occasionally caught him looking and smiled. Afterward, he would ask what was so funny, and the father would answer that he was simply enjoying the view. Their affection followed them beyond the walls of their home because the love they shared was not something either needed to disguise. Still, choosing to keep trying meant understanding that affection alone could not carry every responsibility. They had to continue communicating, honoring boundaries, and recognizing when one of them needed support. The difference was that those responsibilities no longer felt like evidence of a failing relationship. They were becoming part of the life they wanted to build. Their love gave them a reason to keep learning, while their growing maturity helped them care for that love in more intentional ways. They also began recognizing that choosing one another did not mean agreeing on everything or expecting every season to feel effortless. Some days would be busy, some plans would change, and some conversations would require patience. Their commitment was not a promise that neither would ever disappoint the other. It was a shared desire to approach those moments with honesty, tenderness, and respect rather than allowing every difficulty to overshadow the good they continued creating. One evening, as they settled onto the couch, the father’s partner rested his head against his shoulder. The father wrapped an arm around him and kissed the top of his head. “Are you comfortable?” he asked. “Very.” “Good. You’re not going anywhere.” His partner lifted his head and smiled. “That sounds like a threat.” “It’s a promise that you’re stuck watching my terrible television choices.” They laughed, and his partner settled against him again. The moment was simple, yet both understood what made their relationship meaningful. They were not staying together out of obligation, fear, or the belief that they had already invested too much time to walk away. They wanted the companionship they shared. They enjoyed one another. They were attracted to one another, respected one another, and continued finding reasons to be grateful that their lives had crossed. Knowing when to keep trying also means recognizing when a relationship still has something healthy and meaningful to nurture. Love deserves effort when both people are willing to participate, when accountability is possible, and when affection is accompanied by respect. Remaining committed should never require accepting abuse or surrendering personal dignity. Genuine love does not ask someone to disappear so the relationship can survive. It invites two whole people to continue growing while choosing to share their lives. Sometimes the greatest reason to keep trying is found in the ordinary beauty of the person beside you. The laughter, the tenderness, the familiar embrace, the quiet reassurance, and the joy of being chosen are reminders that love is more than surviving difficult moments. Love is also enjoying the life two people are fortunate enough to build together.
