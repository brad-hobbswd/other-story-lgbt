Introduction

There Is More to This Story

There are stories people understand immediately, stories they question, and stories they judge before they have taken the time to understand what is actually happening.

Relationships are complicated enough when two people are trying to understand one another. Add a child, family responsibilities, personal convictions, and the influence of religion, and the circumstances become considerably more complex.

Now imagine two men who love one another and are trying to build a life together. They have responsibilities, expectations, disagreements, and hopes for a future they want to share. They are not exempt from the ordinary struggles that affect relationships. They experience misunderstandings, moments of frustration, differences in communication, and the occasional disappointment that comes when love does not always translate into understanding.

There is also a child whose life is connected to the relationship. That child deserves more than promises. A child needs consistency, reassurance, security, and the opportunity to grow up knowing that the adults in their life can disagree without making love feel conditional.

Then there is faith.

For some people, Christianity is a source of comfort, direction, and hope. For others, the church has become a place where difficult questions remain unanswered and personal experiences are met with judgment rather than understanding.

What happens when two men love one another, believe in Jesus Christ, and still find themselves wrestling with questions about what their faith means for their relationship?

Can someone be gay and genuinely Christian? Can someone worship Jesus, pray, seek forgiveness, pursue a relationship with God, and believe in the promise of Heaven while loving someone of the same sex?

These are not insignificant questions. They are questions that deserve thoughtful consideration, biblical examination, and conversations that extend beyond assumptions.

This book does not pretend that every Christian interprets Scripture the same way. It does not dismiss the convictions of people who hold traditional interpretations, nor does it dismiss the experiences of LGBTQIA+ Christians who believe their relationships can exist within a sincere walk with Christ.

There is a difference between examining a belief and diminishing the person who holds another one.

The same principle applies to relationships.

One person’s experience does not automatically explain everything happening on the other side. A partner may feel neglected while the other believes he is making an effort. One may interpret silence as indifference while the other is struggling to find the words to communicate. Both may feel unheard, even while believing they are trying to be understood.

The presence of a child makes those conversations even more important. Adult disagreements do not exist in isolation. Children observe how people communicate, how they handle disappointment, how they demonstrate respect, and how they respond when circumstances become difficult.

This is not a story about perfect people. It is a story about real people who must examine their choices, their responsibilities, their relationships, and their understanding of faith.

It is also a reminder that Christianity should never become an excuse for cruelty, just as love should never become an excuse for irresponsibility.

Throughout these pages, we will consider the conversations that are difficult to have, the assumptions that create unnecessary distance, the expectations that sometimes-become burdens, and the accountability required to maintain a healthy relationship.

We will also examine a question that reaches beyond the relationship itself.

What does it mean to belong to Jesus Christ?

Ultimately, this is not simply a conversation about two men. It is about how we see people, how we understand relationships, how we interpret faith, and how willing we are to listen when another person’s experience challenges what we have always believed.

There is another side to this story.

Perhaps the first step toward understanding is being willing to hear it.
